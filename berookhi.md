The late-night Mumbai breeze carried the faint, salty tang of the sea up to the balcony, a sharp contrast to the suffocating drama they had just escaped. Priya’s grand party had imploded in spectacular fashion—one reckless betrayal by Kajal, a shattered glass, and a room full of stunned guests watching years of trust unravel in seconds.

Priya stepped out onto the quiet terrace carrying two chilled beer bottles, moving with practiced grace as she gathered the heavy, embroidered layers of her lehenga in one hand, careful not to let the delicate zari snag on the wrought-iron chair or the edge of the sliding door. Exhausted yet buzzing with the sheer shock of the evening, she sank into the seat beside Samarth, fully ready to dissect the entire fiasco over a drink.

He was slumped back in his chair against the night chill, his dark coat pulled up high so that half of his face remained tucked into the shadow of the collar. Only his eyes were fully visible, fixed somewhere out toward the city lights.

She leaned over, nudging the cold bottle against his arm to hand it to him. As he turned to take it, she caught his sidelong glance. It wasn't the look of someone gearing up for juicy gossip. It was heavy, guarded, and devastatingly transparent—a quiet, piercing look that asked a question he would never dare say out loud: *What if you did the exact same thing to me?*

The playful energy drained out of her instantly. Her fingers tightened around the sweating glass of her own bottle until her knuckles turned white, a sudden, fierce heat rising in her chest.

**"Main Kajal nahin hoon,"** she said, her voice low and trembling with quiet fury.

Startled by the sudden sharpness in her tone, he shifted back an inch, blinking as his defensive wall faltered. Hearing the unmistakable hiss of hurt beneath her anger, he let out a breath and raised a placating hand. **"Baba, maine kab kaha tum kaun ho?"**

Her anger refused to budge. She held his gaze, tracing the lines of his eyes in the dim amber glow of the balcony light—and seeing that exact same lingering, unspoken fear still sitting right there beneath the surface.

**"Accha?"** she challenged softly, her grip still locked onto the bottle. **"Toh aise kyun dekh rahe the?"**

He blinked rapidly, schoolboy innocence flooding his dark eyes as he tried to dodge the accusation entirely. **"Main kab dekh raha tha?"** he protested, his voice pitching up just a fraction too high to be convincing.

She didn't buy it for a second. With a sharp, deliberate movement, she leaned forward and slid her bottle back into the plastic beer crate beside her chair, the glass clinking sharply against the empty slots. Moving closer until there was barely any space left between them, she raised a warning finger right in front of his face.

**"Accha, pata hai isse kya kehte hain? Gaslighting,"** she said, her eyes narrowing. **"Therapist ne mana kiya tha na?"**

The moment her finger came up, his gaze dropped straight to it, locking onto the tip as if everything else in the world had suddenly gone out of focus. His restless, overactive mind latched onto the easiest exit ramp available, completely derailing the tension as he looked back up at her with a tiny, nervous trace of a smile tugging at the corner of his mouth.

**"Woh... ek toh tum bekaar ke paise barbaad karti ho uspe,"** he muttered, gesturing vaguely with his chin. **"Like, how many chilled beers can we buy with that? Aur what if *this* is gaslighting, Priya?"**

Before he could finish the thought, her other hand shot out and caught his wrist, her fingers clamping down around his pulse with a sudden, unyielding tightness. **"Bas,"** she warned, low and deadly serious.

Trapped by the wrist, he didn't pull away. Instead, he raised his free hand in agonizing slow motion, extending his index finger toward her still-raised, accusing one—inching closer with the breathless, hyper-focused precision of a man trying to defuse a live bomb wire without setting off the charge.

The sheer audacity of it broke whatever thin thread of patience she had left. **"Kya kar rahe ho tum?!"** she snapped, her voice slicing through the quiet night air.

She stepped in closer, catching both of his hands in hers in a sudden, desperate attempt to pin them still and hold the unraveling moment together—only for the heavy hem of her lehenga to catch beneath her heel.

Her balance gave out instantly. With a sharp gasp, she pitched forward, pulling him off-center with her until they both stumbled back against the cool, textured plaster of the balcony wall with a dull thud.

His breath hitched sharply in his throat. Moving purely on instinct, his hands flew to her waist and shoulders to steady her, bracing her weight against his chest so she wouldn't slip. In the scramble to catch her balance, her palms ended up pressed flat against the front of his shirt, right over the center of his chest.

For a fraction of a second, neither of them breathed.

Slowly, one of his hands lifted from her shoulder, his fingers trembling just slightly as he reached up to gently tuck a loose, stray lock of hair behind her ear. Their eyes locked, and just like that, the rest of Mumbai seemed to vanish. Time simply stopped.

Beneath her palms, she could feel his heart hammering violently against his ribs, matching the wild, erratic thudding of her own. Hers was racing from the leftover adrenaline of her anger and the sudden, breathless shock of being pressed this close to him. His, however, was beating with that same disarming, boyish shyness that still caught him off guard whenever she looked at him like this—even after a full year of living under the exact same roof.

Swallowing hard, he tried to find his voice through the sudden rush of her closeness, clumsiness overtaking every ounce of his usual composure. **"P-Priya, tum..."** he stammered, the words tangling in his throat as a faint flush crept up the back of his neck.

If he expected her to melt, he had severely miscalculated.

Instead of softening, the sharp edge of her fury snapped right back into place, slicing cleanly through the momentary haze between them. Beneath her palms, his frantic heartbeat no longer felt endearing—it felt like a smokescreen. Every single time she cornered him with an uncomfortable truth, he either cracked a joke about chilled beer or pulled this exact card—all wide eyes, stammering vulnerability, and accidental closeness—until she completely forgot what she had been mad about in the first place.

Her jaw tightened, her eyes narrowing into cold, unyielding focus. **"Yeh sab natak mere saamne mat karo,"** she said, her voice dropping into a low, lethal calm that cut right through his stammering. **"Har baar jab tumhein apni galti chhupani hoti hai na, tum yahi karte ho. Pehle mazak, aur jab woh kaam na kare, toh yeh innocent face. Don't think I'm going to forget what I just saw in your eyes, Samarth."**

The words hit him like cold water, stripping away every trace of his earlier flustered shyness. Against the heavy embroidery of her lehenga, his hands resting on her back went completely still—his fingers barely moving, almost numb, as if he couldn't even feel the warmth of her anymore beneath the sheer weight of her accusation.

He didn't pull back, nor did he try to wriggle out of the corner she had backed him into. Instead, his shoulders dropped against the balcony wall, the last of his defenses collapsing all at once. When he finally spoke, his voice was quiet, rough around the edges, and stripped of every bit of playfulness.

**"Accha baba, main galat tha, theek hai?"** he murmured, his dark eyes holding hers without flinching, raw and unguarded. **"Par darr mujhe bhi lagta hai, theek hai? Yeh sirf do din ka pyaar nahin hai, Priya."**

Her breath caught slightly, the sharp edge of her glare faltering as she heard the quiet tremor beneath his words. He swallowed hard, looking down for a split second before forcing himself to look right back at her, laying everything bare in the dim amber glow.

**"Mera dil hai, meri izzat, meri khushi—sab tumhare haathon mein hai,"** he said softly, the words heavy with eight years of shared history, late nights, quiet sacrifices, and everything they had built brick by brick long before they ever moved into this flat. **"Sab tumne kamaya hai aath saal mein. Darr toh lagega mujhe... please."**

For a few long minutes, neither of them moved. A heavy, ringing silence settled over the balcony, broken only by the rustle of the sea breeze against the railing and the steady, rhythmic thud of his heart beneath her palms.

Caught completely off guard by the raw weight of his own confession, Samarth couldn't bring himself to look at her. Still holding her loosely against the wall, he turned his head sideways, fixing his gaze somewhere out into the dark empty air beyond her shoulder—as if meeting her eyes now would strip away whatever tiny shred of pride he had left.

Priya’s fingers twitched against his shirt, trying one last time to tighten, to hold onto the sharp, familiar edge of her anger. Instead, her grip gave way completely, her resistance dissolving like a tomb of sand collapsing at the first touch of water.

Her gaze traced the rigid, clenched line of his jaw before lifting to his eyes. What she saw there made her chest ache. The playful deflection was gone, and so was the boyish shyness; in their place sat a quiet, sinking hopelessness, as though he had already convinced himself that everything he had built over eight years could slip through his fingers in a single night.

Panic flickered behind her ribs. Reaching up with one hand to gently turn his face back toward hers, she tried to pull him out of that dark current with her voice—throwing words out into the silence with the desperate urgency of someone standing on a shore, shouting to bring back a floating plank drifting further and further out into the ocean.

**"Samarth... idhar dekho,"** she said, her voice dropping the sharp edge entirely, trembling slightly as she tried to anchor him back to the present. **"Meri taraf dekho, please. Aise chup mat raho. Jo wahan andar hua, woh humari kahani nahi hai... sun rahe ho tum?"**

Even with the warmth of her palm against his cheek, he refused to meet her gaze. His eyes slid helplessly past her shoulder, latching onto the cluster of potted flowers swaying faintly in the dim corner of the balcony—focusing on the dark leaves and stray petals as if looking at her directly would undo whatever little composure he had left.

**"Mujhe nahin pata, theek hai?"** he muttered, his voice low, rough, and edged with quiet defeat. **"Main koi bhagwan thodi nahin hoon."**

Something inside Priya snapped—not back into anger, but into a sharp, aching hurt that cut straight through her chest.

Without letting him retreat another inch, she shifted herself squarely into his line of sight, bringing her other hand up to frame his jaw so he had nowhere left to look. Gently, firmly, she held his face still until his dark, guarded eyes had no choice but to collide with hers.

Her chest rose and fell rapidly as she searched his face, her voice trembling with a fierce, wounded intensity.

**"Mujhpe bharosa nahin hai?"** she asked, the words spilling out raw and unfiltered. **"Main tumhari kaun hoon, haan? Itna nahin lagta... mujhe nahin pata main kya kar rahi hoon, haan?"**

The balcony fell dead quiet around them, the sea breeze tugging softly at the loose strands of her hair. She didn't blink, holding him trapped in her gaze, waiting—demanding—for him to finally look at her and see that she wasn't drifting away from him at all.
